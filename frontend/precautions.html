Yellow Stream: Infectious & Anatomical Waste

Pathological, anatomical, soiled dressings, expired drugs & clinical lab cultures
Yellow Bag • Biohazard
Audio Narration: Yellow Stream Precautions Audio Ready • Click Play to Listen
0:00 0:45

Typical Waste Items Included

Human tissues & organs Placentas & amputations Soiled gauze & bandages Blood-soaked cotton swabs Plaster casts with blood Expired pharmaceutical medicines Microbiology specimen cultures Discarded live vaccines

Mandatory PPE Checklist

  • Heavy-duty Nitrile / Latex Gloves (Double gloving for pathology)
  • N95 or Certified Fluid-Resistant Surgical Mask
  • Fluid-impermeable plastic apron or disposable gown
  • Protective eye goggles / full face shield
  • Closed-toe non-skid rubber gumboots

Handling & Segregation Standard Operating Procedure

  1. 1 Certified Non-Chlorinated Bags: Always deposit infectious waste into non-chlorinated yellow plastic bags bearing the national biohazard insignia. Never use black or regular garbage liners.
  2. 2 Three-Quarter Fill Limit: Tie and seal bags with zip-ties or swan-neck knots strictly when bags reach 75% capacity. Never overfill or force items inside.
  3. 3 No Manual Compression: Never press down, squeeze, or step on waste bags to create space. Doing so expels infectious aerosol plumes and bursts seams.
  4. 4 Direct Enclosed Transport: Move yellow bags directly via dedicated, sealed biomedical trolleys to the hospital central bio-waste holding area. Never leave bags in ward hallways.
  5. 5 48-Hour Storage Rule: Yellow infectious waste must not be stored beyond 48 hours before high-temperature incineration or authorized common treatment facility dispatch.

Essential Do's

  • Barcode label every yellow bag with ward number and generator timestamp.
  • Autoclave microbiology cultures at 121°C before bagging.
  • Perform thorough 6-step hand hygiene immediately after glove removal.

Critical Don'ts

  • NEVER place needles, scalpels, or glass ampoules in yellow bags.
  • NEVER drag waste bags across hospital floors.
  • NEVER discard chlorinated plastics in yellow stream (produces toxic dioxins).
Regulated Final Disposal Method: High-Temperature Incineration (1050°C) or Plasma Pyrolysis / Authorized Deep Burial

Red Stream: Contaminated Recyclable Plastics

Syringes (without needle), IV sets, catheters, urine bags, dialysis kits & tubing
Red Bag • Recyclable Plastics
Audio Narration: Red Stream Precautions Audio Ready • Click Play to Listen
0:00 0:45

Typical Waste Items Included

Disposable plastic syringes (hub-cut) Intravenous (IV) bottles & sets Foley catheters & drainage tubes Urine drainage bags (emptied) Hemodialysis tubing kits Contaminated examination gloves Blood sample vacutainer tubes Oxygen masks & nebulizer cups

Mandatory PPE Checklist

  • Thick puncture-resistant utility gloves
  • Splash-proof eye goggles or full visor face shield
  • Impervious protective apron
  • Non-slip fluid-resistant boots

Handling & Segregation Standard Operating Procedure

  1. 1 Mutilate / Cut Syringe Hubs: Cut needle tips using point-of-use mechanical needle cutters. Syringes must be rendered un-reusable before dropping into red bags to prevent black market recycling.
  2. 2 Completely Drain Residual Liquids: Completely empty urine bags, IV fluid bottles, and dialyzer lines into hospital sanitary drains before bagging. Never dispose filled liquid containers into red bins.
  3. 3 Pre-treatment Chemical Disinfection: Soak plastics in freshly prepared 1% to 2% Sodium Hypochlorite solution or autoclave for 30 minutes to neutralize infectious pathogen loads.
  4. 4 Segregate strictly from Sharps: Verify zero needle attachments or glass ampoules exist in the red bin. Red plastics go directly to industrial shredders.

Essential Do's

  • Empty all IV fluids and body fluids into sanitary drainage prior to disposal.
  • Use red non-chlorinated plastic bags with biohazard symbol.
  • Ensure shredding occurs only after documented autoclaving/disinfection.

Critical Don'ts

  • NEVER drop needles or scalpels into red bags (severe shredder hazard).
  • NEVER burn or incinerate red plastics (creates carcinogenic fumes).
  • NEVER dispose of general food packaging in red clinical bins.
Regulated Final Disposal Method: Autoclaving / Microwaving / 1-2% Sodium Hypochlorite Disinfection followed by Industrial Shredding & Registered Plastic Recycling

White Stream: Sharps, Needles & Blades

Hypodermic needles, scalpels, surgical blades, lancets & suture needles
White Translucent • Puncture-Proof
Audio Narration: White Sharps Precautions Audio Ready • Click Play to Listen
0:00 0:45

Typical Waste Items Included

Hypodermic injection needles Fixed-needle syringe units Surgical scalpel blades & handles Curved surgical suture needles Blood lancets & micro-needles Needles cut via electric burner Sharp contaminated stylets Broken contaminated glass ampoule tips

Mandatory PPE Checklist

  • Heavy-duty Cut-Resistant Kevlar / Leather Utility Gloves
  • Protective safety glasses with side shields
  • Steel-toe reinforced non-pierce footwear

Handling & Segregation Standard Operating Procedure

  1. 1 Puncture-Proof Rigid Container: All sharps MUST go directly into a rigid, tamper-evident, puncture-proof, leak-proof translucent white sharps container placed right at the bedside.
  2. 2 NO Two-Handed Recapping: NEVER recap needles using both hands! Recapping is the #1 cause of occupational HIV and Hepatitis B infections worldwide. If recapping is unavoidable, use the single-handed scoop technique.
  3. 3 Immediate Needle Destruction: Utilize electric needle burners or mechanical cutters immediately following drug administration to burn or shear the needle hub.
  4. 4 75% Fill Level Cap: When container reaches the marked 3/4 fill line, permanently close the snap-lock tamper-proof seal. Never shake or tamp down sharps.

Essential Do's

  • Keep sharp containers within arm's reach of the injection site.
  • Drop sharps point-down without putting fingers near the opening aperture.
  • Permanently lock the lid before moving the container.

Critical Don'ts

  • NEVER bend, break, or manually strip needles from syringes with bare hands.
  • NEVER place sharps in plastic bags, cardboard, or non-rigid boxes.
  • NEVER retrieve or fish out items once dropped into a sharps container.
Regulated Final Disposal Method: Autoclaving / Dry Heat Sterilization followed by Concrete Encapsulation in Sharps Pit or Metal Foundry Melting

Blue Stream: Glassware & Metallic Implants

Medicine vials, ampoules, microscope slides & orthopedic titanium/steel implants
Blue Box • Glass & Metal
Audio Narration: Blue Stream Precautions Audio Ready • Click Play to Listen
0:00 0:45

Typical Waste Items Included

Glass medicine ampoules (empty) Glass vaccine vials Broken glass laboratory beakers Microscope glass slides & coverslips Orthopedic metallic bone plates Orthopedic screws, pins & rods Metallic prosthetics & surgical clips

Mandatory PPE Checklist

  • Heavy-duty puncture-resistant rubber gloves
  • Safety goggles to shield eyes against glass splinters
  • Protective lab coat or apron

Handling & Segregation Standard Operating Procedure

  1. 1 Rigid Cardboard or Blue Containers: Discard all broken or intact glass articles and metallic surgical implants into cardboard boxes with blue colored markings or rigid blue containers.
  2. 2 Handle Broken Glass with Tongs: NEVER pick up broken glass fragments with bare hands or thin gloves! Always use mechanical forceps, tongs, or a dustpan brush.
  3. 3 Chemical Disinfection Soak: Decontaminate glass and metallic hardware by soaking in 1% Sodium Hypochlorite solution for 30 minutes, or autoclave before handling.
  4. 4 Reinforce Heavy Boxes: Ensure blue cardboard containers are taped securely along bottom seams to prevent glass piercing through during transport.

Essential Do's

  • Rinse medicine bottles with disinfectant if chemical residue is suspected.
  • Keep blue boxes dry to preserve structural cardboard integrity.
  • Route decontaminated glass directly to authorized glass bottle recyclers.

Critical Don'ts

  • NEVER throw glass vials into red or yellow plastic bags (causes bag cuts).
  • NEVER mix cytotoxic chemotherapy vials into regular blue stream.
  • NEVER crush glass manually with bare footwear.
Regulated Final Disposal Method: Autoclaving / 1% Sodium Hypochlorite Disinfection followed by Glass Crushing & Recycling / Metal Smelting

Cytotoxic & Chemotherapy Oncology Waste

Expired antineoplastics, chemotherapy IV sets, contaminated gloves & patient body fluids
Purple / Yellow • Cytotoxic Hazard
Audio Narration: Cytotoxic Waste Precautions Audio Ready • Click Play to Listen
0:00 0:45

Typical Waste Items Included

Expired chemotherapy vials Cytotoxic IV bags & tubing Chemo preparation gowns & masks Spill cleanup absorbent pads Excreta from chemotherapy patients Cytotoxic drug ampoules

Mandatory PPE Checklist

  • Double Gloving: ASTM D6978 Chemo-Tested Nitrile Gloves
  • Impermeable Polyethylene-Coated Gown with closed front
  • N95 / HEPA Respirator Mask
  • Full Protective Face Shield & Goggles

Handling & Segregation Standard Operating Procedure

  1. 1 Cytotoxic Warning Labeling: Bag in heavy-duty purple or yellow bags distinctly marked with the international cytotoxic trefoil symbol and the word CYTO in bold.
  2. 2 Class II Biosafety Cabinets: Reconstitute and prepare all antineoplastic chemotherapy agents only inside certified Class II Biological Safety Cabinets with HEPA air filtration.
  3. 3 On-Hand Spill Kits: Maintain specialized cytotoxic spill kits (containing chemical neutralizing agents, absorbent granules, and specialized tongs) immediately accessible in oncology wards.
  4. 4 Ultra-High Temperature Incineration: Cytotoxic compounds are mutagens and carcinogens; they must undergo incineration at temperatures exceeding 1200°C with secondary combustion chambers.

Essential Do's

  • Change outer chemo gloves every 30 minutes during active administration.
  • Label and seal all cytotoxic bags with double ties.
  • Promptly report any skin or eye contamination to occupational health.

Critical Don'ts

  • NEVER autoclave cytotoxic waste (volatilizes carcinogenic vapors).
  • NEVER discard cytotoxic packaging in regular municipal trash.
  • Pregnant or breastfeeding personnel must NOT handle cytotoxic waste.
Regulated Final Disposal Method: Incineration at temperatures > 1200°C in specialized hazardous waste combustion facilities

Emergency Incident Protocols: Needle-Stick & Blood Spills

Step-by-step immediate first-aid, Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) & chemical spill management

Protocol A: Accidental Needle-Stick / Cut Injury (Step-by-Step)

Step 1 • Immediate

Wash Under Running Tap

Immediately flush the wound under cool running water with mild soap for 5 continuous minutes. Allow blood to flow freely.

Step 2 • Warning

DO NOT Squeeze or Scrub

Never squeeze, pinch, or violently scrub the puncture site. Never suck the wound with your mouth or use corrosive bleach.

Step 3 • Report

Cover & Notify Supervisor

Dry and apply a sterile waterproof adhesive bandage. Report incident to ward sister and the Infection Control Nurse immediately.

Step 4 • PEP Within 2 Hours

Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

Undergo baseline blood tests (HIV, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C). If recommended, initiate ARV PEP treatment ideally within 2 hours.

Protocol B: Blood & Body Fluid Spill Management

Step 1

Cordon Area & Don Full PPE

Place warning caution signs. Don nitrile gloves, protective gown, eye visor, and shoe covers before approaching spill.

Step 2

Cover with Absorbent Towels

Place absorbent paper towels or spill pads over the puddle working inward from edges to center to stop spread.

Step 3

Pour 1% Sodium Hypochlorite

Gently pour freshly made 1% sodium hypochlorite (10,000 ppm chlorine) over towels. Wait 20 minutes contact time.

Step 4

Collect in Yellow Bag & Mop

Use plastic scraper/tongs to deposit soaked towels into a yellow biohazard bag. Mop area with disinfectant.

Hand Hygiene Best Practice

Always perform the WHO 6-step hand washing technique with soap and water for 40–60 seconds, or use an alcohol-based hand rub (70% ethanol) for 20–30 seconds after touching any waste or removing gloves.

• Infection Control Committee: Ext. 4401
• Occupational Health & Needle-Stick Hotline: Ext. 2222
• Bio-Medical Waste In-Charge: Ext. 3310
